# On a phone: parity with desktop CPU, and a resource budget

This records the on-device check of the Android build: does a phone render the same request as desktop CPU, and what
does a read cost there? Measured 2026-11-12 on **one phone**: a Samsung Galaxy Z Fold5 (SM-F946W), Android 16,
arm64-v8a, Snapdragon 8 Gen 2 (CPU parts: 1 Cortex-X3, 2 Cortex-A715, 2 Cortex-A710, 3 Cortex-A510), 4 KB kernel
pages, on USB power. Desktop CPU was an Intel i9-12900K under WSL Ubuntu 22.04.

**Short answer.**
- **Exact parity fails.** The phone and desktop CPU give different tokens and different audio for the same request,
  every time. Each machine repeats itself exactly, so this is arithmetic, not chance.
- **The parity level is distributional.** Across six draws on each machine, the frame counts are not told apart: an
  exact permutation p of 0.22 for Qwen3-TTS, and 1.0 for Kitten-TTS 2.
- **The resource budget fails** on memory, speed and time to first audio, and **passes on heat.**

## What ran

Both machines ran `ggtts-render` built from one commit (`77ec629`, whose engine code is `track/android`'s) with ggml
at `af97976c7810cdabb1863172f31c432dab767de7`, rustc 1.88.0, four threads, CPU backend:

- desktop: `cargo build --release --locked -p ggtts-cli --features ggtts-cli/ggml`;
- phone: `scripts/android_build.sh cargo-build`, cargo-ndk 3.5.4, NDK 27.2.12479018, API 24, NEON in the kernels. It
  ran from `adb shell` in `/data/local/tmp/ggtts`, with `libc++_shared.so` beside it.

Models, the same files on both machines, checked by sha256 on each:

| Engine | Model | Codec | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-TTS | `qwen-talker-hk-cantonese-cc0-v0.0.5-q8_0.gguf` `4eb27e5a…7041`, speaker `hk_voice_343` | `qwen-tokenizer-12hz-q8_0.gguf` `7771c653…7824` | |
| Kitten-TTS 2 | `kitten-lm.gguf` (Q4_0, from `ggtts-kitten-convert`) `263b8e69…a431` | `kitten-s3gen.gguf` `54ad2c36…2321` | a prepared voice, `ba695cc0…d4f4` |

Each render was a two-paragraph story, written for this test, read with the same flags on both machines:

```sh
ggtts-render --model M --codec C [--voice V | --speaker hk_voice_343] --story STORY --threads 4 \
    --trace NAME.trace.jsonl --trace-level 2 --stream NAME.raw.wav --stream-raw \
    --out NAME.flac --timing-report NAME.timing.json
```

**What makes a draw.** On the story path, each chunk's seed comes from the story's title line and the chunk's text;
`--seed` is accepted and ignored ([#215](https://github.com/dandelion-love/ggtts/issues/215)). So "another draw"
here means another title: `Parity`, then `Parity 1` to `Parity 6`, over the same two paragraphs.

`scripts/android_parity.py` compares two runs:
- the trace header (engine, model and codec sha256);
- each chunk's seed and frame count;
- every frame's codebook-0 token, where the engine traces steps (Qwen3-TTS does; Kitten-TTS 2 does not);
- the raw stream's 16-bit samples before the level step.

Its `--self-test` shows identical files reading as identical, and a changed token, sample, frame count or engine
each being caught.

## Parity (AC1)

| Comparison | Qwen3-TTS | Kitten-TTS 2 | What it shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Desktop, the same run twice (no-op) | identical: 140 of 140 tokens, 237,600 samples | identical: 212,160 samples | the comparison reads no change as no change |
| Phone, the same run twice (no-op) | identical: 150 of 150 tokens, 251,280 samples | identical: 212,640 samples | the phone is deterministic |
| Desktop, another draw (control) | differ: first token difference at chunk 0, frame 8 | differ: first sample difference at 1,876 | the comparison can fail |
| **Phone against desktop, title `Parity`** | **differ**: chunk 0, frame 2 (token 212 against 1521); frames [65, 83] against [65, 73] | **differ**: frames [100, 108] against [103, 108]; first sample difference at 1,878 | **no exact parity** |
| Phone against desktop, titles `Parity 1` to `6` | 6 of 6 differ; the first token differs at frame 2 to 10 of chunk 0 | 6 of 6 differ in samples; frames per chunk equal or within 2 | |

The divergence starts in the arithmetic, not in the sampler. At chunk 0, frame 1, the stop probability is
`7.0e-10` on the phone and `0.0` on desktop, with the same token drawn. By frame 2, the sampler draws differently.
For Qwen3-TTS, that makes the phone's read as different from desktop as another draw on desktop is: the control
parts at frame 8, the phone at frame 2. ggml's ARM (NEON) and x86 kernels accumulate in different orders, so
bit-exact output across the two is not something this build provides.

**Distribution parity.** Total frames per run over the six draws (phone, then desktop), with an exact two-sided
permutation test on the difference of means (all 924 relabellings):

| Engine | Phone | Desktop | Means | Difference (phone - desktop) | 95% interval | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Qwen3-TTS | 146, 143, 137, 139, 154, 146 | 162, 160, 152, 155, 141, 135 | 144.2 against 150.8 | **-6.7 frames (-4.4%)** | **-17.5 to about +5** | 0.22 |
| Kitten-TTS 2 | 221, 206, 216, 209, 220, 206 | 221, 205, 218, 208, 220, 205 | 213.0 against 212.8 | +0.2 frames (+0.1%) | -9.3 to +9.7 | 1.0 |

The interval is the set of shifts the permutation test does not reject at 5%. `android_parity.py frames` scans it in 0.1-frame steps, which puts the upper end at +4.9; the exact boundary is +5.0 (p 0.2165 at the observed gap). **Six
draws a side cannot rule out a real gap.** For Qwen3-TTS, the phone could render up to about 17.5 frames (about 12%)
shorter than desktop, or up to about 5 frames longer, and this test would not see it. For Kitten-TTS 2, the frame counts
match draw for draw: within 2 frames in each of 12 chunks.

Per chunk, the phone's frame counts fall inside the desktop's range for Kitten-TTS 2 (chunk 0: 100 to 105 on both;
chunk 1: 104 to 120 on both). For Qwen3-TTS, the phone's chunk 0 runs 59 to 70 frames against desktop's 63 to 78.

**Parity level stated: distribution parity, not exact.** With six draws a side, frame counts do not tell the phone
from desktop. That is a weak test: as the interval shows, it would miss a gap of up to about 12% for Qwen3-TTS, and

they agree only as distributions.

## Resource budget (AC2)

The budget was stated on the issue before measuring. It covers Kitten-TTS 2 (Q4_0) at the binding's default four
threads, over a 5-minute read: a six-paragraph story (29.65 s of audio, 682 frames) rendered with `--stream` as a
listener would hear it. `thermal.sh` sampled every 10 s:
- `dumpsys thermalservice` (the Android thermal status, and the HAL's AP, SKIN and BAT temperatures);
- `dumpsys battery` (the battery temperature).

Peak memory is the process's `VmHWM`, read from `/proc/PID/status` until it exited. `/proc` reports it in kB that are
KiB; GB here are 10^9 bytes. (Corrected 2026-11-12: this record first gave 3.69 GB, reading the KiB as 1000 bytes.) The read ran twice; the first
run's temperature sampler failed, so the temperatures are from the second. Both runs produced the same 682 frames.

| Line | Budget | Measured (run 2; run 1) | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Model memory: peak RSS | ≤ 3.0 GB | 3.78 GB (3,693,672 KiB; 3,694,100 KiB) | **FAIL** |
| Real-time factor | ≤ 1.0 | 11.84 (351.2 s for 29.65 s of audio); 12.60 | **FAIL** |
| Time to first audio, `--stream` | ≤ 2.0 s | 103.9 s; 126.5 s | **FAIL** |
| Thermal status over the read | never above 1 (light) | 0 in all 34 samples, both runs | pass |
| Battery temperature rise | ≤ 8 °C | +3.6 °C (33.6 to 37.2) | pass |

Context for the fails:

- **Memory.** Desktop peaks at 3.74 GB on the same render, so this is the model's size: the LM and the S3Gen decoder
  are 2.54 GB of files, and they are read in whole. It is not a phone effect. Where it goes, and that it is a
  load-time spike: `docs/android/knowledge/research/kitten-memory-and-tq1.md`. The phone had about 3.2 GB available
  before the run, so this read could not run beside a heavy app.
- **First audio.** The first chunk was ready at 47.4 s. `--stream`'s default 5-second level window held it until
  the second chunk, at 103.9 s. On `--stream-raw`, first audio would be the first chunk's 47.4 s, still far over
  budget.
- **Speed.** Generation ran at 2.7 frames per second: 254 s generating and 97 s decoding, of the 351 s. Desktop CPU, at the same
  four threads, runs this engine at an RTF of about 5.4. The phone is about 2.4 times slower than desktop at the
  same thread count.
- **Heat.** The SoC sensor (AP) rose from 34.5 °C to a peak of 51.0 °C at 70 s, then settled at about 47 °C. The skin
  sensor rose 5.6 °C. Android never raised the thermal status.

Qwen3-TTS, for comparison (six draws, not budgeted): peak RSS about 2.97 to 3.02 GB (first given as 2.90 to 2.95), and an RTF of 6.7 to 7.2 for about
10.3 s of audio (desktop: 2.5 to 2.9).

## What this does not show

- One phone, one Android version, one build. Another SoC, kernel or NDK may round differently again.
- Distribution parity is tested on frame counts only, with six draws a side. Nothing here was listened to, and no
  measure of audio similarity was taken.
- The phone was on USB power, so battery drain was not measured. Its temperatures started warm (33.6 °C battery),
  from earlier runs.
- The budget was measured through `ggtts-render` in `adb shell`, not through the app or the binding. The binding's
  story path salts its seeds with the render's seed, so the app's draws differ from these. The cost of a draw should
  be the same, but that was not measured.
- Four threads only. The phone has eight cores; more threads were not tried.

## To repeat

Build both binaries as above and push the Android one, `libc++_shared.so`, the stories and the scripts in this
record's PR to `/data/local/tmp/ggtts`. On the phone, run `parity.sh 20260905`, `dist.sh 6` and `run-budget.sh`.
Each stops and says so when a render fails, and `thermal.sh` stops when a field cannot be read. (The runs recorded
here passed `--seed 20260905`; the story path ignores it, so `parity.sh` no longer passes it.) On desktop,
run the same flags. Then:

```sh
python3 scripts/android_parity.py compare PHONE_DIR DESKTOP_DIR qwen-t1 kitten-t1    # and the other runs
python3 scripts/android_parity.py frames PHONE_DIR DESKTOP_DIR 'qwen-t*'
```
